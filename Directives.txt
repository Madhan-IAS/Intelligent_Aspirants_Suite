
UPSC Mains Question Types & What They Mean & How to write

Directive / Keyword	What You Should Do	Depth Required
Comment	Give your opinion based on facts + balanced arguments	Medium
Examine	Investigate closely → reveal facts, causes, implications	High
Critically Examine	Same as examine + mention positives & negatives before a conclusion	High
Discuss	Present multiple viewpoints + wider explanation	Medium–High
Critically Discuss	Multi-dimensional view + critical analysis + conclusion	High
Elaborate / Explain	Make the idea clear with details, how & why	Medium
Briefly Explain	Only essential points with short clarity	Low–Medium
Evaluate	Assess value, pros & cons, judging impact + final verdict	High
Analyze	Break issue into components (causes, effects, stakeholders)	High
Critically Analyze	Analysis + highlight limitations + judgment	Very High
Justify	Prove the statement correct with strong arguments & examples	Medium
Substantiate	Support the argument with data/facts/reports/examples	Medium–High
Illustrate	Explain with examples, diagrams if possible	Medium
Enumerate	List out points systematically (no deep explanation needed)	Low
Differentiate / Distinguish	Show clear differences using comparison table	Medium
Elucidate	Explain clearly with clarity, like teaching someone	Medium
Assess	Estimate impact, significance, or value	High
Analyze the statement	Break down the meaning + comment if true/not true	High
To what extent do you agree?	Give your stance with justification + counter-view	High
Critically Evaluate	Evidence-based judgment + strengths & weaknesses + conclusion	Very High
Do you agree? Give reasons	Take a stand and defend it logically	Medium
Suggest Measures / Way Forward	Provide feasible solutions	Medium
Highlight	Focus only on key significant points	Low–Medium
Throw light on	Provide main features and significance	Medium

Shortcut for Understanding Levels

Level	Directive
Low	Enumerate, Highlight
Medium	Comment, Explain, Illustrate, Justify
High	Discuss, Evaluate, Analyze, Assess
Very High	Critically Discuss/Evaluate/Analyze

Pro Tip for UPSC Writing

✔ Define the concept
✔ Break into dimensions (political, economic, social, environmental, etc.)
✔ Give examples/data/committees
✔ Add challenges + solutions
✔ End with a strong conclusion

✅ Directive-Wise Answer Writing Structure for UPSC Mains

1️⃣ Comment

Intro: Define the issue
Body: Your balanced viewpoint + facts
Conclusion: Short personal assessment
> Balanced + analytical opinion

2️⃣ Discuss
Intro: Context + definition
Body: Multi-dimensional explanation (political, economic, social, etc.)
Conclusion: Sum up with future outlook
> Cover wide aspects, not deep critique




3️⃣ Critically Discuss

Intro: Define + context
Body:
  ✔ Positives / significance
  ✔ Negatives / challenges
  ✔ Support with examples / data
Conclusion: Balanced judgment
> Always include both sides

4️⃣ Examine
Intro: Define topic
Body: Probe causes, facts, functioning
Conclusion: What does the examination reveal?
> Bring hidden facts to light

5️⃣ Critically Examine
Same as above + mention gaps/limitations clearly.
> end with: “Despite challenges, it remains essential …”

6️⃣ Analyze
Intro: Short intro
Body: Divide into components (causes → effects → stakeholders)
Conclusion: What is the key takeaway?
> Break down in logical parts

7️⃣ Critically Analyze
Add pros + cons + finalize stance in conclusion.

8️⃣ Evaluate
Intro: Define objective
Body: Weigh pros vs cons + evidence
Conclusion: Final judgment based on analysis
> Give a “value judgment”
9️⃣ Critically Evaluate
Same as Evaluate + point out shortcomings clearly
> “However, it falls short in…”

🔟 Explain / Elaborate / Elucidate
Intro: Define
Body: Clear logic — How? Why?
Conclusion: Essence in one line
> Very clean, simple, clarity-focused

11️⃣ Briefly Explain
Same as above but 2–3 strong points only
> No deep details


12️⃣ Justify

Intro: Rephrase the statement
Body: Provide strong arguments proving it correct
Conclusion: Reinforce correctness
> Do NOT include counterpoints, UPSC wants **support**

🎯 Which Value-Add to Use for Which Question Type?

Add-on	Best Suited For	Avoid In
Maps (India/world)	Geography, IR, Environment, Security (border issues), Disaster mgmt	Ethics, Economy theory, Polity legal topics
Data & Reports (NITI, NCRB, WHO etc.)	Governance, Economy, Social issues, Health, Education	Philosophical questions
Articles / Constitutional values	Polity, Governance, Rights issues, Federalism	Pure Economy or Env issues
SDGs / NDC Goals / Global conventions	Environment, Sustainability, Social development	Historical evaluation
Case Studies (best practices in states, schemes)	GS2 & GS3 (governance, agriculture, women issues)	Geography pure theory
Judgments (SC landmark cases)	Rights, federalism, criminal justice reforms	Economy/Geography
Schemes & Committees	Direct govt intervention topics	Ethics paper
Quotes	Ethics (GS4), Society topics	Hard data questions
Flowcharts & Diagrams	All questions if time permits	Only if you don’t understand the concept 😅

Sample Questions and answers for the different directives for easy understanding of the answer writing format

1️⃣ Discuss
Q: Discuss the challenges and opportunities associated with India’s Demographic Dividend.
India, with 65% of its population below 35 years, is entering a period where the working-age population surpasses dependents. This demographic transition offers a rare opportunity for accelerated growth, similar to East and Southeast Asia.
Opportunities
•	Economic Growth Driver: If skilled & employed, youth can add 2% extra GDP growth annually (UNFPA).
•	Innovation & Startups: India is 3rd largest startup ecosystem → youth-led entrepreneurship.
•	Domestic Consumption: Expanding middle class supports manufacturing & services.
•	Strategic Advantage: Young military workforce strengthens internal & external security.
Challenges
•	Skill Mismatch: Only 4.7% formally skilled (NSDC).
•	Jobless Growth: Automation & informal sector dominance.
•	Low Female LFPR: ~24% → gender gap limits potential.
•	Health & Nutrition: High anemia (57% women) → low productivity.
Diagram — Simple Cause–Effect
Demographic Dividend → Human Capital → Higher Productivity → Growth
↑ Employment, Skills, Health Intervention needed
Way Forward
•	Align education with Industry 4.0 needs — NEP 2020
•	Boost labour-intensive sectors (MSME, textiles)
•	Women workforce integration → childcare support
•	Public health + nutrition mission convergence
Conclusion
If leveraged wisely, India’s demographic dividend can become a demographic powerhouse—realizing economic & constitutional goals of justice, equality & dignity. Else, it may turn into a demographic liability.

2️⃣ Examine
Q: Examine the role of GST in strengthening India’s fiscal federal framework.
Goods and Services Tax (GST) replaced multiple indirect taxes creating a unified national market. It introduced cooperative federal decision-making through the GST Council.
Strengths
•	Cooperative Federalism: Centre + States jointly frame rates and rules.
•	Revenue Buoyancy: Taxpayer base doubled to 1.4+ crore; e-compliance reduced evasion.
•	Reduced Cascading Tax Effect: Input tax credit supports Make in India exports.
•	Improved Ease of Doing Business: Uniformity → reduced logistics cost by 20%.
Issues
•	Compensation Dispute: Post-2022 states fear revenue instability.
•	Centre Dominance: Voting structure 33% (Centre) + states divided → imbalance.
•	Compliance Burden: Small traders struggle with monthly returns.
Table — Impact Summary
Parameter	Status
Tax Buoyancy	Strong
Fiscal Autonomy	Partial concern
Federal Harmony	Improved but fragile
Way Forward
•	Extend compensation support model
•	Simplify slabs, support MSMEs
•	More fiscal autonomy over rates for states
Conclusion
GST has shifted India toward economic federalism, but sustained fiscal trust is crucial to deepen cooperative federalism envisioned in Art. 279A.

3️⃣ Evaluate
Q: Evaluate the effectiveness of MGNREGA in strengthening rural livelihoods.
MGNREGA ensures 100 days of wage employment as a legal right, covering 7–8 crore households yearly.
Achievements
•	Social Safety Net: During COVID-19, highest employment demand met.
•	Women Empowerment: 55% women participation, boosting financial inclusion.
•	Asset Creation: Water conservation, rural infrastructure → long-term productivity.
•	Poverty Reduction: Reduced seasonal distress migration; supports landless labourers.
Limitations
•	Delayed Payments: >35% payment delays reduce trust (CAG).
•	Quality of Assets: Weak monitoring → durability issues.
•	Regional Imbalance: Better implementation in southern states.
•	Political Interference: Ghost beneficiaries, corruption.
Flowchart — How MGNREGA helps livelihoods
MGNREGA → Income → Consumption ↑ → Local Markets ↑ → Rural Growth ↑
Way Forward
•	GIS-based asset tracking
•	Convergence with agriculture, irrigation schemes
•	Strengthen grievance redressal & digital payments
•	Regular indexation of wages to inflation
Conclusion
MGNREGA remains India’s most impactful rights-based livelihood programme. To reach its full potential → efficiency + accountability must rise alongside expenditure.

4️⃣ Critically Examine
Q: Critically examine the role of MSP in ensuring food security in India.
MSP has been a crucial state mechanism supporting farmers and ensuring procurement for the Public Distribution System (PDS).
Positive Contributions
•	Food Security Backbone: 80 crore people under NFSA depend on subsidized grains.
•	Rural Income Security: Predictable prices during market failures.
•	Price Stabilization: Prevents farmer distress sales.
•	Strategic Buffer: For droughts & emergencies.
Criticisms
•	Crop Bias: 85–90% MSP procurement restricted to rice-wheat belt → Punjab, Haryana.
•	Environmental Harm: Groundwater depletion, stubble burning.
•	Fiscal Burden: Subsidy crossed ₹2.2 lakh crore → unsustainable.
•	Market Distortion: Does not incentivize diversification into pulses/oilseeds.
Mini Diagram
High MSP → Overproduction of Wheat/Rice → Soil/Water Stress → Climate Risk ↑
Way Forward
•	Decentralized Procurement: Promote millets, pulses (India is Millet Mission leader).
•	Price deficiency payments (Madhya Pradesh model)
•	Strengthen e-NAM, private storage & FPO marketing
•	Water-saving technologies (micro-irrigation)
Conclusion
MSP ensured food security → SDG-2: Zero Hunger, But India must shift towards nutrition security + farmer income security + ecological sustainability.

5️⃣ Evaluate
Q: Evaluate the performance of MGNREGA in reducing rural distress.
Intro
MGNREGA, launched in 2005, aims to ensure livelihood security through 100 days of wage employment.
Evaluation structure → Show merits + demerits + judgment
✔️ Positives
•	Safety net during crises (COVID-19 migrant returnees)
•	Women’s participation ↑ ~55%
•	Assets creation: water conservation, rural roads
•	Reduction in seasonal migration in drought-prone areas
❌ Limitations
•	Delayed payments, wage rates below real market wages
•	Leakages in muster rolls → corruption
•	Asset quality inconsistent → limited long-term economic value
Data / Reports
•	World Bank calls MGNREGA “world’s largest social security program”
•	Rural distress improved but not eliminated
Judgment
MGNREGA contributes to poverty reduction and social inclusion, but strengthening governance, digital monitoring, and skill-linked assets is urgent.
Conclusion
It remains essential for rural resilience and must evolve into a productivity-enhancing developmental instrument.
